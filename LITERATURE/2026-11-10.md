## Implications for SESPy

**Chen et al. (Systems 2026)** is the most directly on-topic paper this week. Its group model building + CLD + loop detection + social ecological model framework is identical to SESPy's target workflow. Specific observations:

- 26 variables, 11 loops, explicitly labelled reinforcing and balancing — SESPy's `find_loops()` and `loop_polarity()` cover this exactly.
- Leverage point identification using the social ecological model layered on top of the CLD — SESPy's leverage composite and `leverage_depth` column (shipped as #23) covers this.
- Participatory co-construction via Community Advisory Board over multiple iterative sessions mirrors SESPy's intended user workflow.
- **This paper validates existing functionality.** No new algorithm is introduced; purely applied modelling in a food-security / urban health domain.
- Non-actionable.

**Garcia et al. (ERIS 2026)** strengthens the theoretical grounding for SESPy's shipped governance metrics (`governance_gap`, `governance_actor_influence`, `governance_concentration`). Its finding that institutional archetypes with higher polycentric governance (wider participation, more actors) show better dynamic fitness provides indirect empirical support for the polycentric concentration index (#26, shipped). No new computational contribution is proposed; non-actionable.
