## Implications for SESPy

**Stochastic token diffusion on a participatory CLD (key paper).** Donlan et al. present a decision-support framework that combines three steps — participatory causal loop diagramming, stochastic token diffusion modelling, and network centrality analysis — applied to small-scale octopus fisheries in Peru and Chile. The token diffusion step answers a question SESPy currently cannot: "If I intervene at node X, how do the effects propagate through the CLD — which nodes receive influence, how quickly, and with what net sign?" Tokens (units of perturbation) are seeded at an intervention node and spread stochastically along directed edges; edge signs determine whether a token flips polarity as it travels; accumulation at each node after N steps gives a ranked reach-and-speed profile for each candidate intervention. The approach is qualitative (no edge-weight magnitudes needed), lightweight (hundreds of random walks per simulation), and naturally handles branching paths and competing feedback loops — all within SESPy's stated scope. It is distinct from all existing and open features: `leverage_scores()` ranks nodes by static centrality independently of any source; `causal_paths()` (#16) enumerates structural paths but does not simulate propagation; `uncertainty_scores()` perturbs structure, not dynamics. A `token_diffusion(g, source_node, n_steps, n_tokens)` function in `sespy/dynamics.py` would give SESPy a lightweight *intervention simulation* capability grounded in a peer-reviewed SES application.

**Governance network connectivity in watershed SES management.** Rojas et al. use a spatial agent-based model of the Coello River Basin (Colombia) to show that institutional network connectivity among stakeholders drives forest conservation and water regulation outcomes more strongly than formal land-use zoning. This is an empirical validation of the governance connectivity concept operationalised in SESPy's shipped `social_ecological_fit` metric and the open issues #13 (governance gap) and #14 (governance actor influence). No new algorithmic extension is suggested beyond those issues; the paper reinforces their value.

**Entropy partitioning for open flow networks.** Merlin & Ramousse propose a Shannon entropy framework that separates internal flow disorder, boundary exchange diversity, and dissipative losses into normalised descriptors, applied to 74 empirical systems (ecosystems, cities, industrial networks). The result — a two-dimensional "organisation–adaptability window" — identifies where 84.4 % of natural ecosystems cluster. The framework requires actual quantitative flow magnitudes (throughput, input/output rates), which SESPy's qualitative CLDs do not carry. Non-actionable for SESPy's static qualitative scope.

**Participatory Systems Mapping Design Framework.** Rigby et al. present a structured deliberative tool for researchers choosing and designing participatory systems mapping sessions, built around three considerations: project scope, added value of participation, and capacity constraints. Directly validates SESPy's participatory CLD-first workflow (stakeholders co-develop the causal map, then SESPy runs network analysis on it). No computational extension.

### Medium
- **NEW — `sespy/dynamics.py` — stochastic token diffusion intervention simulation.** Add `token_diffusion(g, source_node, n_steps=10, n_tokens=1000, seed=None)` that seeds `n_tokens` unsigned perturbation units at `source_node` and propagates them through the CLD over `n_steps` via random walks: at each step a token at node v picks an outgoing neighbour w uniformly at random; tokens traversing a negative edge flip their sign. After the run, collect per-node statistics: total tokens received, net sign (majority positive/negative/contested), and step of first arrival. Return a DataFrame sorted by total tokens received, surfaced in the app as a collapsible "Intervention Simulation" section with a source-node selector and step/token controls. *Motivated by:* A systems thinking approach to improve participatory processes in small-scale fisheries management (10.21203/rs.3.rs-10397797/v1). Opened as razinkele/SESPy#17 (2026-08-04).
